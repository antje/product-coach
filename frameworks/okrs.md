# OKRs: the bridge from strategy to execution

Strategy → OKRs → Roadmap → Execution. A strategy nobody can act on is just a document. Teams need to know exactly what they're optimizing for and why.

## Shape

- **One Objective** that flows directly from the Playing to Win cascade. Inspiring, qualitative, in the customer's terms.
- **Three Key Results**, each a measurable **outcome**: `metric: baseline → target by deadline`.

## The outcome-vs-output rule (the rule that matters most)

- ❌ **Output / delivery milestone:** "Ship readiness model by Q2" measures activity. You can hit it and still fail the user.
- ✅ **Outcome:** "Race-week retention 34% → 62%" is a real change in user behavior, the result the strategy promised.

Test each KR: *if we shipped everything on the roadmap and this number didn't move, would we have failed?* If shipping alone satisfies the KR, it's an output in disguise.

## Quality bar for each KR

1. Names a **metric**, a **baseline**, a **target**, and a **deadline**. A KR missing its baseline is unfalsifiable.
2. Is an outcome (behavior/result), not an output (delivery).
3. **Traces to the strategy.** You can point at the cascade row (usually How to Win or Winning Aspiration) this KR proves out. A KR that doesn't trace is a gap between strategy and plan.
4. Is ambitious but anchored. The target has a reason, not a round number.

## Reference example (HealthTrack)

Objective: become the app serious recreational athletes trust to make their race-week decisions.
- KR1: 7-day race-week retention 34% → 62%
- KR2: readiness-model opens per user 1.2 → 4.5
- KR3: race-week NPS 31 → 58

## Accountability (from the six-step process)

OKRs without owners, kill criteria, and a review cadence are decoration. Each Objective needs: an owner, the ritual where it's reviewed (with a cadence), and the condition under which the bet gets killed or revisited.
