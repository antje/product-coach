# The one hard no

**Strategy is a set of choices about what you will not do. If it says yes to everything, it hasn't made a choice.**

Most "strategies" are wish lists in a nicer font: a vision, a stack of goals, and a roadmap where everything is a priority. The moment a strategy accommodates everyone, it has stopped being a strategy.

## Form

> We will not ___ because ___.

One deliberate trade-off — not a list of deprioritized items, and not something you were never going to do anyway.

## Quality bar

1. **Valuable** — the thing refused must be genuinely tempting: revenue, a loud customer segment, an obvious adjacency. Refusing something worthless is not a choice.
2. **Deliberate** — the *because* explains what the no protects (focus, margin structure, trust, architecture), not just why the thing is hard.
3. **Singular** — one no. A list of no's is deprioritization, not strategy.
4. **Load-bearing** — removing the no should visibly weaken How to Win. Figma's "no desktop app" forced the collaboration-first architecture that was the strategy. Notion's early "no enterprise depth" protected the product's simplicity.
5. **Uncomfortable** — the discomfort writing it is the signal it's real. If you can't name a hard no, you have goals, not a strategy.

Example: *"We will not expand into grocery or convenience delivery as part of this initiative, because it is a different business model with a different margin structure."*
