# Growth hypothesis & the bet — distilled rules

A growth push is only as good as the belief underneath it. These rules force that belief into a form that can be proven wrong.

## Product-led growth, the premise

In PLG the product itself is the main vehicle for acquisition, activation, retention, and monetization. Before buying growth (ads, sales headcount), ask what the product could do to earn it. The default answer "spend more on acquisition" is usually a way to avoid a harder activation or retention problem.

## PLG, SLG, or both — the conversion hypothesis

- **PLG:** the product is the sales team. Users experience value before they pay; that experience converts. Self-serve, low-friction, high-volume, low-touch.
- **SLG:** relationships are the sales team. Guided to value through outreach and negotiation. High-touch, enterprise, custom pricing.
- **The hybrid reality:** mature companies don't choose. PLG proves value at scale and surfaces high-intent users; sales closes them at exactly the right moment. The product identifies who is ready.
- The emerging third motion: AI-led growth, where the LLM does the qualifying and guiding.

Name the motion before designing the funnel; the motion decides where conversion is supposed to happen.

## The PLG engine playbook

Six steps, run as a loop, not a checklist — each feeds the next and the motion compounds: **map your funnel → focus on one key area → anticipate challenges → define activation → convert your users → build PLG muscle.** The bet below is steps 2 through 4 done honestly.

## Funnel vs loop

- A **funnel** is linear: traffic in, conversion out, and it decays the moment you stop feeding it.
- A **growth loop** compounds: a user's action produces an output that brings in the next user, whose action produces more output.
- Loop families: **viral** (users invite users), **content/UGC** (usage creates public artifacts that acquire), **collaboration** (the product is better multiplayer, so users pull in coworkers or counterparties), **paid** (revenue funds acquisition that generates revenue).
- Pick the loop the product's usage naturally feeds. A loop bolted onto behavior that doesn't exist is a referral program, not a loop.
- One user who serves many others (an accountant, an agency, a team lead) is a multiplier node: the highest-leverage entry point for a collaboration loop.

## The hypothesis format

> **IF** we [make one specific change] **THEN** [named metric] moves from [baseline] to [target] **BECAUSE** [the causal mechanism, stated as a belief about user behavior] **MEASURED BY** [the exact metric and window].

Rules that make it a hypothesis and not a wish:

1. One change. If the IF clause has an "and", it is two experiments.
2. Baseline and target are numbers, from data, not vibes. No baseline, no hypothesis.
3. The BECAUSE clause is the actual bet. If it is wrong, the metric will not move even if the change ships perfectly. Write it so it can be wrong.
4. MEASURED BY names something a user does, not something a user sees.

## The bet

The bet is the **smallest experiment that can falsify the belief** — not the roadmap that assumes it.

- State what you will build, for whom, and the single decision the result feeds.
- State **what you are deliberately not doing** and why (usually: more acquisition spend, pricing changes, downstream mechanics). The not-dos are load-bearing; without them the bet is unbounded.
- A bet you cannot lose is not a bet. Name the result that would kill it.

## Constraint diagnosis (which lever first)

Look at where the numbers refuse to move despite effort. Conversion flat while traffic and usage grow points at **activation**. Retention decaying by cohort points at **habit**. Everything healthy but revenue flat points at **monetization**. Bet on the constraint, not the lever you like.
