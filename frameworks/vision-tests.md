# Three tests for a defensible vision

A vision states who we serve, what problem we solve, and why now. It sits above strategy and should be stable while strategy adapts. Before building strategy on a vision, it must pass all three tests.

## 1. Why now? (market timing)

What shifted in the last 2–3 years that makes this the right moment? If the vision would have been equally valid five years ago, it is a description, not a vision.

*Example: Zoom's async-video bet paid off when remote work became the default in 2020.*

## 2. Why us? (competitive position)

What do we understand about this market that others haven't seen yet? A durable right to win — genuine asymmetry, not enthusiasm.

*Example: Figma — design as a team sport, and only credible browser-native.*

## 3. Why us, now? (organizational readiness)

What would need to be true inside the org for this to be achievable? Tension between ambition and readiness is healthy; a complete disconnect is fatal.

*Example: a 12-person team committing to own one city by year-end has a vision; the same team declaring itself the global standard has ambition without an anchor.*

## Using the tests

- Apply each test as a direct question and demand a one-sentence answer. Vague answer → failed test.
- A vision statement that can't settle a team disagreement is not specific enough.
- Failing "why us, now?" doesn't kill the vision — it defines the capability gap that belongs in the strategy's Capabilities row.
